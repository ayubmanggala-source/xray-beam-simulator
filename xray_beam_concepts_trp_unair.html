X-Ray Beam & Radiation Types

Conceptual animation of beam divergence and radiation pathways

Select a radiation type to highlight its pathway, or choose Show All.

Primary Radiation

Useful X-ray beam emitted through the tube window and collimator, before interaction with the patient. It diverges from the focal spot.

Secondary Radiation

Radiation produced during interactions with matter. This diagram emphasizes scattered radiation, which travels in different directions.

Remnant Radiation

Radiation emerging from the patient and reaching the detector, including transmitted primary photons and some scattered photons.

Leakage Radiation

Radiation escaping through the protective tube housing by paths other than the intended beam exit window. Shown exaggerated for teaching.

Educational concept visualization only. Radiation paths are schematic; photon interactions and intensities are not calculated. Leakage is illustrated to explain its origin, not its typical magnitude. The detector receives remnant radiation, not all incident primary radiation.
